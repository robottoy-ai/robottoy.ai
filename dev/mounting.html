How parts hold onto the shell

One joint, drawn in cross-section. This exact stack repeats ~30 times inside the penguin, and it is the same joint inside every gadget you own.

1. The joint, zoomed in

Why the brass matters: a screw cut straight into plastic strips after 2-3 services. The insert's knurls are melted into the plastic permanently, and steel-in-brass threads survive hundreds of services. The screw clamps the part's ear flat onto the boss top — the part cannot slide, lift, or rattle.

2. A servo on two bosses

The flanges ("ears") on every servo exist precisely for this. The motor's torque goes through the screws into the shell — never through glue or friction.

So "everything on brass inserts" really means: the heavy and the moving things screw into brass; the light things sit in shaped pockets; the battery is padded and captive, touched by no screw. Insert kits come with the iron tip included; setting all ~30 is an evening.
