Why the one-motor waddle walks

Front view, our real dimensions. The rule of all walking: the weight (dot) must be over the foot that is on the ground. The crank rocks the body so the weight settles over one foot; the other foot is then weightless and swings forward free. Drag the sliders and find where it falls.

Numbers behind it: feet centers ±35 mm, each sole 34 mm wide, so the weight must shift 18 mm sideways to free a foot. At 73 mm weight height that needs atan(18/73) = 13.9° of rock — exactly what toy penguins visibly do. Energy to pump one rock: ~0.008 J; the N20 motor delivers hundreds of times that. Crank torque worst case ~0.8 kg·cm vs the motor's 2+ kg·cm. And the existence proof: "passive dynamic walkers" — the 1938 Wilson Walkie penguin toy — walk down a gentle ramp with no motor at all. The physics does the walking; our motor only replaces the ramp.
